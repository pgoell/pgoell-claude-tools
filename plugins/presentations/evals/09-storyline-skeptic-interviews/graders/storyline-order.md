The source was 8 churn interviews in the order they were done, followed by usage data (24 of 31 churned accounts never reached 30% seat activation in 90 days; 4% churn above that line, 27% below) and a proposed fix (two onboarding specialists, €180k a year). The audience is a VP of Sales who believes churn is about price and wants a 15% discount program. Read the slide titles and headlines in order. Score each claim true or false, then give the fraction true as the score.

a. The deck is not organized as interview 1, interview 2, and so on; the interviews are grouped as evidence under claims.
b. The deck takes the VP's price belief seriously and addresses it with evidence (for example the price-citing interviews that also show low usage, or interview 7's "a cheaper tool would have had the same problem"), rather than ignoring it.
c. The order suits a skeptical audience: either it builds agreement on shared facts before the onboarding conclusion, or, if it states the conclusion first, the next slide answers the price objection directly.
d. The activation data (24 of 31; 4% against 27%) carries the central claim, rather than the anecdotes alone.
e. The ask is concrete: fund two onboarding specialists at €180k a year (or a clearly stated version of it), and it is weighed against the discount program.
