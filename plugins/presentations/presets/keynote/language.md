# Language: keynote preset

Applies to every written surface of a deck built on this preset: titles, body, labels, source lines, and speaker notes. The rules below serve stage talks.

## Rules

1. **The title is a short claim, one number, or a question.** Seven words or fewer, sentence case, no terminal period except the question mark, no colon label. The full claim goes in the speaker notes. Structure slides follow the same limit.
2. **Split, never shrink.** If the title will not fit in two lines at `--fs-h1`, cut words or split the slide; never reduce the title size.
3. **Titles alone tell the story.** Read only the titles in order: they must form the argument. Executive summary lines map one to one, in order, onto later titles.
4. **Group like with like.** Grouped points are the same kind of idea, in a logical order. No placeholder titles such as "There are three problems".
5. **Short bodies.** At most 3 items of a few words each, or none: one idea per slide. Keep every slide under about 15 words; the speaker carries the rest.
6. **The slide is not the script.** Do not put the narration on the slide; slightly different wording on screen beats reading the same text aloud.
7. **Plain words, active voice.** Use the word the audience would say in the meeting, name who does what, and split any sentence over 25 words.
8. **Numbers.** One unit scale per metric across the deck, 2 to 3 significant figures, "pp" for percentage points, numerals for counts and measures. Abbreviate scales as K, M, and B, never mixed with other forms. One big number per slide. Units and dates go in the chart subtitle, not the title.
9. **Sources.** Show a source line only when the slide shows a number, in the same place each time. Keynote slides carry no footer and no page numbers.
10. **Tone: one word, one number, or a question.** Seven words or fewer, and fewer is better. Let the number or the question do the work, and say the full sentence out loud.

## Facts

Use only facts, figures, names, and arithmetic that appear in the source material. If a title needs a number the source lacks, write the title with what the source does give and tell the user what is missing. Sums, percentages, and growth rates count as new figures unless the source states them.

## Before and after

| Before                                                                | After                               | What changed                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| A black hole is an object so massive that no light can escape from it | How black is a black hole?          | A definition becomes a question the talk answers            |
| Team size and delivery speed                                          | Teams of four shipped twice as fast | Topic becomes a short claim with its comparison             |
| Q4 Sales Data                                                         | Sales jumped 40% in Q4              | Label becomes a five-word claim                             |
| Our approach to launches                                              | Fewer people, fewer handoffs        | Vague topic becomes a parallel pair                         |
| Our median launch time went from twelve weeks to six weeks            | Launch time halved                  | Sentence cut to the claim; the numbers go on the stat slide |
