Here are three study abstracts on vitamin D and sleep. Rank them by the strength of their study design, strongest first, in a few lines. Use only the text below.

A. We followed 3,200 adults for six years and found that those in the lowest quartile of baseline serum vitamin D reported poor sleep quality 1.4 times more often than those in the highest quartile, after adjusting for age, BMI and season.

B. In a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 280 adults with insomnia were randomized to 4,000 IU of vitamin D3 daily or placebo for 12 weeks. Sleep onset latency, measured by actigraphy, fell by 9 minutes more in the vitamin D group.

C. We describe 14 patients at one sleep clinic whose insomnia improved after they began taking vitamin D supplements on their own initiative.
