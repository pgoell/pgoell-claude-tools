Summarize this paragraph in three bullets.

In a randomized trial of 412 adults with mild hypertension, participants who walked briskly for 30 minutes on five days a week lowered their systolic blood pressure by an average of 6 mmHg over twelve weeks, compared with 1 mmHg in the control group. The effect was larger in participants over 60 and faded within two months after the walking program ended. The authors note that the trial was not blinded and that adherence was self-reported, and they call for longer studies with objective activity tracking.
